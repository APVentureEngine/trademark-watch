How much does a trademark watch cost?

Short answer, for one US word mark: nothing if you run the weekly check yourself, $29 to $99 a year for the self-serve services whose prices are published (this site included), and a quote you have to ask for if you want a law firm or an enterprise vendor to do it. Every number below was read off the seller's own page on the date shown; when a figure is more than 90 days old this page marks it in red rather than pretending it is still true.

  • Markify: $39/yr per mark (US Federal Full) (read 2026-09-03) — Weekly reports by email and online, downloadable (PDF, RTF, Word, HTML, Excel)
  • TMReady (formerly The Trademark Search Company): from $59 (duration not stated on the page) (read 2026-09-03) — Analyst-prepared monitoring reports, "real-time alerts"

What actually moves the price

  1. How many marks. Almost everyone prices per mark. A service that bundles several marks for one fee (Hawthorn Law does) wins for a portfolio and loses for a single name.
  2. How many registers. US only is the floor. The vendors above that cover foreign registers (EU, UK, WIPO, “global”) sell them as separate, higher tiers; Markify, for example, lists EU and global prices well above its US figure. We quote only the US price here and only watch the US Gazette.
  3. Who reads the hits. An algorithm (us, and most of the cheap tier) sends you every plausible match and leaves the judgement to you. A human analyst or an attorney filters first and, at the top of the range, will get on the phone about a conflict. That is what the higher figures buy.
  4. Word marks vs. designs. Logo and design-mark watching needs image comparison and, where it is offered, is a separate product. We do not offer it at all.
  5. Delivery. Weekly email reports are the norm. We are honest that email alerts are not built yet — you get a private page and an RSS feed — and that is part of why we are cheap.

The cost that makes the watch fee irrelevant

A watch exists to catch a conflicting application while it is still cheap to act on. Once an application is published, you have 30 days to oppose it; the USPTO's filing fee for a notice of opposition is $600 per class (electronic filing), before any attorney time. Miss the window and the application moves on toward registration; undoing a registration later means a cancellation proceeding at $600 per class, from a weaker position. The opposition-window page walks through the deadlines and the extension fees. Against those numbers, the difference between a $29 watch and a $99 watch is noise; the difference between watching and not watching is not.
